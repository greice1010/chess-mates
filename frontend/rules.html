Rules of Chess

Goal

Checkmate the opponent's king: attack it so that it cannot escape capture.

Setup

Each player has 16 pieces: a king, a queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. White moves first, then players alternate.

How pieces move

  • King ♔ – one square in any direction.
  • Queen ♕ – any number of squares in straight or diagonal lines.
  • Rook ♖ – any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
  • Bishop ♗ – any number of squares diagonally.
  • Knight ♘ – an "L": two squares one way, one square perpendicular. It can jump over pieces.
  • Pawn ♙ – forward one square (two from its start square); captures one square diagonally forward.

Special moves

  • Castling – king moves two squares toward a rook and the rook jumps over it, if neither has moved and no squares between are attacked or occupied.
  • En passant – a pawn may capture an enemy pawn that just advanced two squares, as if it had moved one.
  • Promotion – a pawn reaching the last rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight (this game auto-promotes to a queen).

Check, checkmate and draws

A king under attack is in check and must escape immediately. If it can't, it's checkmate and the game is lost. A game is drawn by stalemate, insufficient material, threefold repetition or the fifty-move rule.
